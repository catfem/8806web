# 8806web

example for dropshipping to FRC 8806 marketing deparatment. ( yes is mocking but for educational purpose)

A concept fundraising website for **FRC Team 8806 — Our Lady of Providence Dream League (OLPDL)**, built from the team's 2025 sponsorship proposal. It's a static site with no build step: open it, edit it, and host it anywhere (GitHub Pages works out of the box).

## What's on the page

The page is ordered by the questions a sponsor asks: *are they good → what do I get → is it a good cause → who are they → what does my money buy → how do I give*. Nothing is scroll-jacked: no pinning, no scroll-snapping, no hijacked wheel and no parallax. The nav, the right rail and the phone dock are fixed, everything else scrolls normally, and the scroll-linked drawing (see *Motion* below) only follows where you scroll. On desktop the mouse wheel glides a little (Lenis, see *Smooth scrolling* below); touch screens and reduced motion keep the browser's own scrolling.

## Look

A dark, technical product-page style (one content column framed by 1px rules), laid out as the team's own engineering drawing set. The page styles live in `assets/css/main.css`, the drawing layer in `assets/css/cad.css` and the motion in `assets/css/motion.css`; the tokens are on `:root` in each.

- **Palette:** charcoal page `--bg #121316`, raised surface `--bg-2`, hairline rules `--rule`, text `--text` / `--muted` / `--dim` (every text colour is at least 4.5:1 on every surface; nothing on the page is set below 11 px), and one accent, cobalt blue `--accent #2a6df4` (with the lighter `--accent-text #6aa5ff` when the accent is text on the dark page), used sparingly: one word in a headline, the number badge, active states, chart bars, award markers and the focus ring. Team-travel bars are hatched in the same blue, so they differ from the build bars by pattern. The 3D robot matches: blue-alliance bumpers, a blue datum ring and a blue rim light. The team navy only appears in the crest and the 3D scene.
- **Frame:** the column is at most 1280 px wide (with at least an 80 px empty gutter on the left while the rail shows) with full-height rules on both sides and small "+" ticks where section rules cross them. Every section starts with a 44 px strip (`.strip`: "02 / RESULTS" on the left, a mono meta line on the right) and every block inside a section is separated by a full-width rule. No rounded corners (the crest is a round disc), no shadows, no blur.
- **Type:** Archivo (variable, expanded `font-stretch: 112%`, weight 800) for headlines — sized in container units (`cqi`) so they follow the column, which the rail narrows; JetBrains Mono, uppercase and tracked, for short labels, nav, buttons, captions, strips and numbers; Inter for body text and for anything that reads as a sentence (scoreboard labels, notes, the legal line). Chinese uses Noto Sans TC (900 for headlines) with less tracking on mono labels.
- **Drawing layer** (`cad.css`): each section is a sheet and its strip the sheet header. One 1px line weight; grey for geometry (`--cad` for dimension and leader lines, `--cad-faint` for centre, construction and hidden lines), blue and white only for facts and actions. Linetypes: long-dash-dot centre lines (`--ln-center`, the hero crest), dashed hidden lines for what isn't built yet or isn't counted (the goals, the off-season rows of the event record), and a 135° section hatch (`--hatch-section`) on the 2026 stat panel, whose small labels sit on solid chips. Each season's event record is a revision table (mono header, tabular figures, one rule per event) whose awards sit on numbered balloons. From 1100 px, zone letters (A, B, C… skipping I and O, one per 320 px) run down the drawing border beside each section (`initZones()` in `assets/js/cad.js`; left gutter only while the rail shows). Buttons, price tags and the cards (reasons, benefits, subteams, Impact numbers) show four square selection grips on hover and keyboard focus; the zone under the reading line is lit (the zone cursor), a hovered or focused event row's rule turns blue, the budget dimension that matches the pressed scope is blue, and a hovered parts-list row lights its cart balloon (state changes only, so they work with reduced motion too). One dimension style (closed 8×6 px arrowheads, extension lines 3 px off the object) dimensions the budget bar, and item balloons (`balloon()` in `cad.js`) tie the pledge list, the cart and the 3D price tags together. Every number in an annotation comes from `data.js` or the live camera. All of it is decorative: `aria-hidden`, no pointer events and never focusable. What sits on static content is drawn without JavaScript; the balloons and the 3D view's corners come with the JavaScript-rendered parts list and viewer.
- **Motion** (`assets/css/motion.css`, `assets/js/motion.js`): *the drawing is plotted as you read it.* The page is the CAD session that plots Drawing Set 8806, with three drivers. **Scroll is the pen:** linework only (the drawing border, sheet rules, BOM column rules and leaders, the budget's dimensions, the 2026 hatch) is drawn 1:1 by your scroll through CSS scroll-driven animations; it moves only while you scroll, stops when you stop and un-draws on the way back up. Never text, never a fact. **Arrival regenerates:** text arrives once, in 1 s or less, and never re-hides. **Touch picks:** hover and focus select, clicks answer. The reading line sits 35% down the screen (`PEN` in `motion.js`, the same line the rail uses).
  - **Plotter pen and zone cursor:** a 2px blue trace runs down the left drawing border from the current sheet's "+" to the reading line and ends in a 5×5 pen head (≥761 px); on phones a 2px bar under the nav grows across the current sheet. From 1100 px the zone letter under the reading line turns white with a 12px blue tick ("you are in 03-B").
  - **Sheet registration:** as a sheet boundary rises to the reading line, its "+" ticks turn in square and a blue cut runs along its top rule; the sheet header types itself in behind a block caret (id, then meta, within 1 s), and the title block's sheet number rolls up to the new one.
  - **Toolpath headlines:** a laser toolpath (a 1px blue edge with a 7px tool head) cuts each of the seven section headlines in, line by line. Never the hero headline.
  - **Odometer counts:** non-money counts (the scoreboard if it was below the fold, the Impact numbers, the 2026 award count) are read off spinning wheels; the real number stays in the page throughout and nothing beside it moves. A number on screen when the page opens never rolls.
  - **Records plotted row by row:** each season's event record is plotted as it arrives: a pen line along each event's rule, its cells left to right, its award balloons in order. Hovering a row, or focusing its event code, lights its rule. The 2026 stat panel is cut in section by the scroll, leaving its hatch behind.
  - **Selection grips:** reasons, benefits, subteams and the Impact numbers select like CAD entities on hover and focus (four blue grips, the index tag turns blue).
  - **The robot regenerates on entry:** its parts converge and the camera swings about 20° into its datum view as the viewport scrolls in, then it stays assembled; the tab bar slides and the view label rolls.
  - **BOM and budget measured by scroll:** the bill of materials is machined row by row at a pen line 75% down the screen (column rules, item balloons, leaders); the budget's dimensions measure the full-season bar as it rises to mid-screen, and the scope toggle lights and re-measures the dimension its line items add up to. Bars keep a one-shot plot (bar lengths encode money).
  - **Pledge chain:** + rolls the quantity, the item balloon flies into its cart line (on phones into the dock's total while it shows) and pings, a new line slides in, the first time the pledge becomes sendable "Send via Instagram" arms, and the confirmation note places. Hovering or focusing a BOM row lights its cart line's balloon.

  Browsers without CSS scroll-driven animations (or `?sda=0`) get the one-shot plotter for the linework (ticks snap, a blue sweep runs along each strip, the BOM balloons pop, the budget dimensions draw once) plus a small JavaScript trace for the pen. Also still there: the frame drawing itself on a fresh desktop load and the hero crest plotting (Detail A, about 1.1 s), blocks fading up in small batches, the map's flight arcs, the rail and nav indicators sliding, steppers pressing and the pledge totals ticking. Never animated: the hero headline, sub-copy, buttons and partners, and money in any form. Tokens (`--t-micro` 160 ms … `--t-long` 1.2 s, `--t-type` 14 ms a typed character, `--row-step` 70 ms between record rows, `--stagger`, `--rise`) are on `:root` in `motion.css`, with one easing family (`--ease` quart-out, `--ease-io` only for travel between two rests); only transform and opacity move (plus the crest's stroke-dashoffset and state colours).
- **Components:** square buttons (`.btn--primary` white, `.btn--ghost` outlined), cell rows (`.cells`: bordered cells sharing their borders, used for partners, goals, tabs, the budget toggle (toggle buttons with `aria-pressed`) and chips), bordered grids (`.grid`: 1px gaps over the rule colour), photo|text rows (`.split`), and the badge + wordmark.

## Run locally

ES modules need a web server (opening `index.html` as a file won't work):

```bash
python3 -m http.server 8000
# then open http://localhost:8000
```

## Deploy on GitHub Pages

1. Merge this branch into `main`.
2. In the repo, go to **Settings → Pages**, then set **Source: Deploy from a branch**, **Branch: `main` / `(root)`**.
3. The site will be at `https://<user>.github.io/8806web/`.

## Editing content

- **Numbers, budget, sponsor items, seasons, calendar, exchanges, outreach, media, sponsors, contacts:** `assets/js/data.js`. The budget totals and the chart are computed from the line items there.
- **Counts that follow the data:** the award and season counts ("11 awards. 5 seasons." in the headline, the *Proven* reason, the scoreboard and the page title) and the outreach / exchange counts are computed from `SEASONS`, `OUTREACH` and `EXCHANGES`, so adding a season updates them. The number of regionals (9) is the number of official `events`, and the list of places ("New Taipei City, Hawaii, Istanbul, Arizona and Shanghai") comes from their `place`; the map labels ("Istanbul · 4 regionals · İstanbul & Bosphorus 2024 · Haliç & Marmara 2026") are computed from the same events plus each place's `exch` teams. The newest-season scoreboard cell, the award balloons ① to ⑪, the best-qualification-run line and the outreach year range are computed too. The title block's revision is one letter per season with the latest year ("E · 2026"), so it moves on with `SEASONS` too. Only the static `<title>` and `<meta>` description in `index.html` (what crawlers and link previews read before JavaScript runs) need a manual edit.
- **Seasons:** each `SEASONS` entry has a `year`, the `game`, a photo (`img`, `imgAlt`), an optional `note` ("Rookie season"), its official regionals in `events`, its off-season events in `offseason` (shown in the table under a dashed rule, never counted as FRC awards) and, where confirmed, `record` (the official W-L-T including playoffs). Each event has the FRC Events `code` (off-season: the TBA `key`), `name`, `short` (map labels), `venue`, `place`, `dates`, `teams` (teams that played qualification matches), `rank` (the final qualification rank, or `null` where it isn't confirmed — the cell then shows "—"), `wlt` (qualification record), `alliance` (`{ n, role }`, role `captain` / `pick1` / `pick2` or `null` where it isn't confirmed), `result` (built with `RESULT.winner()`, `finalist()`, `out()`, `third()`, or written out with its own `sub` line), `awards` (from `AWARD`, which holds the exact FRC Events names, any "sponsored by …" / "in honor of …" part in `by`, and the Chinese names from the official 2026 雙北盃 plan) and an optional footnote `note`. A season with `draft: true` is hidden and left out of the award counts — use it to prepare results before they are confirmed. A season with no `img` gets a branded placeholder tile (the newest gets the stat panel).
- **Outreach:** an `OUTREACH` entry can carry a `note` (shown in smaller text under the event).
- **Page copy:** `index.html`. Each translatable element has its English text inline and the Chinese in a `data-zh="…"` attribute next to it.
- **Contact settings** (`SITE` in `data.js`):
  - `email`: leave it empty to hide the e-mail buttons. Fill it in to enable "Send by e-mail" for pledges.
  - `proposalUrl`: link to the sponsorship proposal PDF (for example `assets/proposal-2025.pdf`). When it's set, the contact block shows a "Download our sponsorship proposal (PDF)" button; empty hides it.
  - `facebook`: currently a Facebook *search* link. Replace it with the page URL.
  - `conceptNotice`: shows "Concept site … not an official team page" in the footer. Set it to `false` once the team adopts the site.

## Using the team's real CAD model

The built-in robot is generated in code (no download). To show the team's actual robot instead:

1. In Onshape, export the robot assembly as **GLB**.
2. Compress it for the web (this usually takes a 30–100 MB CAD export down to a few MB):
   ```bash
   npx @gltf-transform/cli optimize robot.glb assets/models/robot.glb \
     --compress meshopt --texture-compress webp --simplify true
   ```
3. Set `robotModelUrl: 'assets/models/robot.glb'` in `assets/js/data.js`.

A custom model is auto-scaled and centred. It keeps the three camera views and gets a generic exploded view in the "price" tab. The mechanism animations and the 3D price tags and sponsor panel only exist on the built-in model, so with a custom model the price tab lists the price tags as buttons and the logo tab shows the sponsor panel as a 2D card (the same drawing as on the built-in robot).

## How the 3D viewer works

`main.js` → `initShowcase()` wires the three tabs (they switch the text even without WebGL) and lazy-loads `scene.js` when `#robot` is about one viewport away. `scene.js` → `initShowcase({ canvas, wrap, section, hotspotLayer, hotspots, onHotspot, onStage, onCamera, sponsors, modelUrl, reducedMotion, lang, view })` returns `{ custom, setView(id), setName(str), setLang(l), redrawText() }`.

- Each tab is a row in the `VIEWS` table (camera orbit, target, mechanism pose, price-tag opacity, sway). `setView` blends every field over ~0.9 s; with reduced motion it snaps.
- The "Your logo here" view frames the sponsor panel using the model's `panel` anchor when there is one. The panel is drawn by `assets/js/sponsor-panel.js` on a 1024×1366 canvas: every supporter at one size (long names wrap onto two lines rather than shrink), and a typed name in a highlighted slot on top.
- Price tags are placed beside their part and pushed apart when they would overlap each other or another part's dot, and kept 44 px from the top and bottom (phones: 40 px and 48 px) so they clear the corner labels. A leader runs from the part's dot to the tag, wherever the tag was pushed. Each hotspot's `ref` is its balloon: an item number from `ITEM` in `main.js` (derived from the parts list order) or a sheet number. `onHotspot(h, { keyboard })` tells the page whether a tag was activated from the keyboard, so focus follows to the parts list only then.
- `onStage(id)` reports each finished build stage — `'frame'`, `'drive'`, `'electronics'`, `'elevator'`, `'effector'` (the labelled pauses of `buildRobotAsync`), then `'intake'`, `'shading'` (after the shader warm-up) and `'done'` (a custom model reports `'model'`, then `'done'`) — which drives the "Regenerating · n/7" status line. `onCamera(az, el)` reports the camera's azimuth and elevation in whole degrees, only when one of them changes.
- The robot assembles as it scrolls into view (once; a deep link that lands on it plays the short timed version): while the viewport rises into the screen, the parts converge and the camera swings about 20° (`ENTER_SWING`) into its datum view with the scroll (quart-out of how far it has entered, from geometry cached on resize, never read per frame); once the whole viewport is on screen it stays assembled whatever the scroll does. If the view is already in place on its first frame, the parts converge over 1.1 s instead. A tab picked during the scrub tweens on from there and ends it for good. Not with reduced motion (assembled at the datum view), and not if another tab was picked first.
- Start-up is spread over idle time so scrolling stays smooth: the engine starts once the main thread is quiet after `#robot` comes within a viewport, the robot is built in chunks (`buildRobotAsync` in `robot-model.js`), and the shaders are compiled before the first visible frame (in parallel where the GPU supports it, otherwise a few at a time).
- The canvas is sized by a `ResizeObserver`, the pixel ratio is capped (1.5 on phones, 2 otherwise), phones get a lighter model (`detail: 'low'`), and the render loop only runs while `#robot` is on screen and the tab is visible.
- No WebGL: a photo is shown instead, the price tab lists the price tags as buttons and the logo tab draws the sponsor panel in 2D, so the name preview still works. No JavaScript: the photo and all three tab texts are shown.

## Performance & accessibility notes

- Three.js is vendored (`assets/vendor/three`, r169, MIT) and only requested when you scroll near the robot section, so the hero doesn't pay for 3D (even the WebGL support check waits until then). There is one WebGL canvas on the page.
- Web fonts never block the first paint: Archivo, Inter and JetBrains Mono load in the background in one request (`media="print"` swap, `display=swap`), and Noto Sans TC is only requested, the same way, when the page is shown in Chinese.
- Photos are WebP (~1.6 MB total) and all lazy-loaded; the hero shows only the crest mark (16 KB, also the nav logo). They're small originals, so no photo is shown wider than about 1.3× its native width.
- Motion never holds content back: every hidden starting state and every scroll timeline hangs off `html.mo`, which the head script only sets without reduced motion, and a 3 s backstop removes it if `motion.js` hasn't started (so a failed script can't leave blocks invisible). Without JavaScript, in print and with reduced motion the final page paints first. The scroll-driven parts (`html.sda`, set where `animation-timeline: view()` is supported) are linework or covers only, never a fact; text is hidden at most briefly by its one-shot entrance (a record cell 750 ms, a headline line 760 ms, a sheet header 1 s). In-page jumps (`html.is-jumping`), deep links and Back / Forward land on a finished drawing: scroll-linked linework is mapped to the position, and headers, records and headlines reached on the way are simply there.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off camera sway, spinning parts, view transitions, the robot's entry scrub and assembly intro (it is assembled at its datum view), the boot and all draw-ins, every scroll-linked drawing (no pen trace or nav bar, no cuts, the BOM and budget drawn), the typed headers, toolpaths, odometers and row-by-row records, the reveal fades, the pledge flight, quantity roll, arming and note, the marquee (a manual scroller, no Pause button), the map's arc flight and halo pulses, the season clock's blink, smooth scrolling (native, instant anchors), and the slides of the rail bar and the tab bar. What stays, as instant state changes: the zone cursor, the record row pick, the card and button grips (opacity only), the budget's lit dimension, the tab bar's position, the sheet number and the view label, and the cross-probed cart balloon. Switching it on while the page is open stops the motion (the 3D robot included), removes any rolling wheels or flying balloon, and stops the smooth scrolling; switching it off starts the photo strip.
- Nothing moves on its own for more than 5 s without a way to stop it (WCAG 2.2.2). The scroll-linked drawing (the pen, cuts and ticks, the BOM, the budget, the 2026 hatch, the robot's entry scrub) is driven by your scroll and stops the instant scrolling stops; the one-shot entrances (typed headers, toolpaths, odometers, records) take 1 s or less and never repeat; grips, the row pick and the cross-probe exist only while hovered or focused; the tab bar, the budget's re-measure and the pledge chain finish within about 1 s of the click. The photo strip, the only endless motion, has a Pause / Play button and pauses while hovered and off screen; the map halos pulse twice and the season clock blinks three times once it is on screen; the 3D robot's idle sway, elevator cycle and spinning rollers ease to rest about 5 s after the last drag, tab change or arrival in view. No caret blinks and no pen head pulses.
- Numbers already on screen when the page opens (the hero scoreboard) never roll; only numbers scrolled into view do, and their real value stays in the page (the odometer is an `aria-hidden` overlay). Keyboard focus that reaches a block (or a season's record) before its reveal shows it at once. Every new motion node (traces, the nav bar, wheels, the flying balloon, the tab bar) is `aria-hidden` and never focusable.

### Smooth scrolling

On desktop (a fine pointer that can hover, no reduced motion, no data-saver) `motion.js` loads [Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis) 1.3.26 (vendored in `assets/vendor/lenis`, imported through the import map) as soon as the page is interactive and the browser has a quiet moment (it is `modulepreload`ed on desktops only), and the mouse wheel glides (`lerp` 0.1). Wheel targets are kept on whole pixels: Lenis 1.3.26 never finishes a glide that ends on exactly half a pixel. It drives the real window scroll, so sticky elements, every observer, find-in-page, back / forward and deep links like `?lang=zh#budget` behave as usual. Touch screens never load it.

- Every in-page link goes through `goTo()` (`motion.js`): a short eased glide (0.6–1.2 s, by distance) that lands the section under the nav, updates the URL hash (back returns to where you were) and moves keyboard focus to it. The price tags use it too (the BOM row is centred) and the camera tag jumps to the budget. Without Lenis (phones, reduced motion) the browser scrolls (smooth, or instant with reduced motion), and the hash and focus still follow. If something above the target changes size on the way (an image, the robot's no-WebGL stand-in), the landing is corrected at the end.
- The keyboard and focus win: Page Up / Down, Home, End, Space and the arrow keys, and focus moving by Tab, stop a glide and scroll natively. The phone menu keeps its own scrolling (`data-lenis-prevent`).
