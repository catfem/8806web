# 8806web

example for dropshipping to FRC 8806 marketing deparatment. ( yes is mocking but for educational purpose)

A concept fundraising website for **FRC Team 8806 — Our Lady of Providence Dream League (OLPDL)**, built from the team's 2025 sponsorship proposal. It's a static site with no build step: open it, edit it, and host it anywhere (GitHub Pages works out of the box).

## What's on the page

The page is ordered by the questions a sponsor asks: *are they good → what do I get → is it a good cause → who are they → what does my money buy → how do I give*. Nothing is scroll-jacked: no pinning, no scroll-snapping, no hijacked wheel and no parallax. The nav bar and the phone dock are fixed, everything else scrolls normally, and the little scroll-linked linework (see *Motion* below) only follows where you scroll. On desktop the mouse wheel glides a little (Lenis, see *Smooth scrolling* below); touch screens and reduced motion keep the browser's own scrolling.

## Look

**Studio:** the team presented like a flagship product launch, with the restraint of a professional product page. A near-black page, solid panels with crisp hairline borders, big tight sans headlines, the robot as the product shot, and engineering precision kept as fine detail (hairline callouts, mono labels, balloons and dimensions). Nothing glows: no backdrop blur, no coloured shadows, no light pools, no gradient fills — which also keeps the page cheap to draw on low-end phones. The page styles live in `assets/css/main.css`, the fine detail in `assets/css/cad.css`, the motion in `assets/css/motion.css` and the shop's own parts in `assets/css/shop.css`; the tokens are on `:root` in each.

- **Palette:** near-black page `--bg #0a0b0d`, flat panels `--bg-2` / `--bg-3`, translucent hairlines `--rule` / `--rule-2` / `--rule-3`, text `--text` / `--muted` / `--dim` (every text colour is at least 4.5:1 on every surface; nothing on the page is set below 11 px), and one accent, cobalt blue `--accent #2a6df4` (with the lighter `--accent-text #7aaaff` when the accent is text on the dark page), used for facts, actions and state, never as a glow: one word in a headline, the number badge, the current nav link, active states, top rules on the newest season and the closing panel, chart bars, award markers and the focus ring. Team-travel bars are hatched in the same blue, so they differ from the build bars by pattern. The 3D robot matches: blue-alliance bumpers and a blue datum ring. The team navy only appears in the crest and the 3D scene.
- **Layout:** one column at most 1240 px wide, no frame rules; sections are separated by generous space (`--sec-gap`) and open with a small chapter label (`.strip`: a blue dash, "02 / RESULTS", and a mono meta line on the right). Blocks are rounded panels and cards (`--r-xl` 28 px, `--r-lg` 20 px, `--r-md` 14 px, `--r-sm` 10 px) with a hairline border and a soft shadow (`--shadow`); photos are rounded too.
- **Type:** Geist (variable; 600 for headlines, tight negative tracking, sized in container units so they follow the column) for headlines and text, Geist Mono, uppercase and tracked, for small labels, captions, chapter labels and data. Chinese uses Noto Sans TC (700 for headlines) with less tracking on mono labels.
- **Fine detail** (`cad.css`): each season's event record is a revision table (mono header, tabular figures, one rule per event) whose awards sit on numbered balloons, with the off-season events under a dashed rule (a hidden line: shown, never counted; the goals use the same dashes). One dimension style (1px `--cad` lines, closed 8×6 px arrowheads, extension lines 3 px off the object) dimensions the budget bar, and item balloons (`balloon()` in `assets/js/cad.js`) tie the pledge list, the cart and the 3D price tags together. The 3D view's corners carry the drawing view, the regenerate status and the live camera azimuth / elevation. Every number in an annotation comes from `data.js` or the live camera. Hover and focus light what they touch: a card's border lifts into the light and its index number turns blue, a hovered or focused event row's rule turns blue, the budget dimension that matches the pressed scope is blue, and a hovered parts-list row lights its cart balloon (state changes only, so they work with reduced motion too). All of it is decorative: `aria-hidden`, no pointer events and never focusable.
- **Motion** (`assets/css/motion.css`, `assets/js/motion.js`): *calm, like a product page.* Things arrive once and settle slowly; nothing loops on its own except the photo strip. Three drivers:
  - **Arrival:** text arrives once, in 1 s or less, and never re-hides. On load the product shot eases up out of a slight zoom (1.6 s) and its five callouts draw out from their dots one after another; the headline, sub-copy and buttons never move. The seven section headlines rise line by line out of their masks; blocks fade up 18 px in small batches; chapter labels rise in with their section. Non-money counts (the scoreboard if it was below the fold, the Impact numbers, the 2026 award count) are read off spinning odometer wheels; the real number stays in the page throughout and nothing beside it moves, and a number on screen when the page opens never rolls. Each event row of a season's record places as that row arrives (a blue line along its rule, its cells left to right, its award balloons in order).
  - **Scroll:** a little fine linework follows your scroll 1:1 through CSS scroll-driven animations and un-draws on the way back up: the parts list's column rules and item balloons, and the budget's dimensions as the full-season bar rises to mid-screen. Never text, never a fact; bars keep a one-shot plot (bar lengths encode money). The robot assembles as its stage scrolls in: its parts converge and the camera swings about 20° into its datum view, then it stays assembled.
  - **Touch:** the robot's lit tab pill slides and the view label rolls; the budget's scope toggle lights and re-measures the dimension its line items add up to; in the pledge, + rolls the quantity, the item balloon flies into its cart line and pings when that line is on screen (above 900 px, where the cart sits beside the list; at 900 px and below the cart is stacked under the list and the dock stays hidden over it, so the flight is usually skipped and the quantity roll is the feedback), a new line slides in, the first time the pledge becomes sendable "Send via Instagram" arms (an outline steps out from it once), and the confirmation note places.

  Browsers without CSS scroll-driven animations (or `?sda=0`) draw the same linework once (the BOM balloons pop, the budget dimensions draw). Also there: the map's flight arcs, the nav's current link and reading hairline, steppers pressing and the pledge totals ticking. Never animated: the hero headline, sub-copy, buttons and partners, and money in any form. Tokens (`--t-micro` 160 ms … `--t-reveal` 900 ms … `--t-long` 1.2 s, `--row-step` 70 ms between record rows, `--stagger`, `--rise`) are on `:root` in `motion.css`, with one easing family (`--ease` quart-out, `--ease-io` only for travel between two rests); only transform and opacity move (plus the callouts' clip and state colours).
- **Shape:** four radii (`--r-xl` 16 px panels, `--r-lg` 12 px cards, `--r-md` 8 px buttons and inputs, `--r-sm` 6 px small controls), one subtle `--shadow`, and 1px borders everywhere.
- **Components:** buttons (`.btn--primary` solid white, `.btn--ghost` outlined), segmented controls (`.cells`: options on one track, used for tabs, the budget toggle (toggle buttons with `aria-pressed`) and chips), card grids (`.grid`: cards with a gap), photo|text panels (`.split`), labels on the 3D stage, and the badge + wordmark.

## Editing content

- **Numbers, budget, sponsor items, seasons, calendar, exchanges, outreach, media, sponsors, contacts:** `assets/js/data.js`. The budget totals and the chart are computed from the line items there.
- **Counts that follow the data:** the award and season counts ("11 awards. 5 seasons." in the headline, the *Proven* reason, the scoreboard and the page title) and the outreach / exchange counts are computed from `SEASONS`, `OUTREACH` and `EXCHANGES`, so adding a season updates them. The number of regionals (9) is the number of official `events`, and the list of places ("New Taipei City, Hawaii, Istanbul, Arizona and Shanghai") comes from their `place`; the map labels ("Istanbul · 4 regionals · İstanbul & Bosphorus 2024 · Haliç & Marmara 2026") are computed from the same events plus each place's `exch` teams. The newest-season scoreboard cell, the award balloons ① to ⑪, the best-qualification-run line and the outreach year range are computed too. Only the static `<title>` and `<meta>` description in `index.html` (what crawlers and link previews read before JavaScript runs) need a manual edit.
- **Seasons:** each `SEASONS` entry has a `year`, the `game`, a photo (`img`, `imgAlt`), an optional `note` ("Rookie season"), its official regionals in `events`, its off-season events in `offseason` (shown in the table under a dashed rule, never counted as FRC awards) and, where confirmed, `record` (the official W-L-T including playoffs). Each event has the FRC Events `code` (off-season: the TBA `key`), `name`, `short` (map labels), `venue`, `place`, `dates`, `teams` (teams that played qualification matches), `rank` (the final qualification rank, or `null` where it isn't confirmed — the cell then shows "—"), `wlt` (qualification record), `alliance` (`{ n, role }`, role `captain` / `pick1` / `pick2` or `null` where it isn't confirmed), `result` (built with `RESULT.winner()`, `finalist()`, `out()`, `third()`, or written out with its own `sub` line), `awards` (from `AWARD`, which holds the exact FRC Events names, any "sponsored by …" / "in honor of …" part in `by`, and the Chinese names from the official 2026 雙北盃 plan) and an optional footnote `note`. A season with `draft: true` is hidden and left out of the award counts — use it to prepare results before they are confirmed. A season with no `img` gets a branded placeholder tile (the newest gets the stat panel).
- **Outreach:** an `OUTREACH` entry can carry a `note` (shown in smaller text under the event).
- **Shop:** `assets/js/shop-data.js` (see *The team shop*).
- **Page copy:** `index.html`. Each translatable element has its English text inline and the Chinese in a `data-zh="…"` attribute next to it.
- **Contact settings** (`SITE` in `data.js`):
  - `email`: leave it empty to hide the e-mail buttons. Fill it in to enable "Send by e-mail" for pledges.
  - `proposalUrl`: link to the sponsorship proposal PDF (for example `assets/proposal-2025.pdf`). When it's set, the contact block shows a "Download our sponsorship proposal (PDF)" button; empty hides it.
  - `facebook`: currently a Facebook *search* link. Replace it with the page URL.
  - `conceptNotice`: shows "Concept site … not an official team page" in the footer. Set it to `false` once the team adopts the site.

## Using the team's real CAD model

The built-in robot is generated in code (no download). To show the team's actual robot instead:

1. In Onshape, export the robot assembly as **GLB**.
2. Compress it for the web (this usually takes a 30–100 MB CAD export down to a few MB):
   ```bash
   npx @gltf-transform/cli optimize robot.glb assets/models/robot.glb \
     --compress meshopt --texture-compress webp --simplify true
   ```
3. Set `robotModelUrl: 'assets/models/robot.glb'` in `assets/js/data.js`.

A custom model is auto-scaled and centred. It keeps the three camera views and gets a generic exploded view in the "price" tab. The mechanism animations and the 3D price tags and sponsor panel only exist on the built-in model, so with a custom model the price tab lists the price tags as buttons and the logo tab shows the sponsor panel as a 2D card (the same drawing as on the built-in robot).

## How the 3D viewer works

`main.js` → `initShowcase()` wires the three tabs (they switch the text even without WebGL) and lazy-loads `scene.js` when `#robot` is about one viewport away. `scene.js` → `initShowcase({ canvas, wrap, section, hotspotLayer, hotspots, onHotspot, onStage, onCamera, sponsors, modelUrl, reducedMotion, lang, view })` returns `{ custom, setView(id), setName(str), setLang(l), redrawText() }`.

- Each tab is a row in the `VIEWS` table (camera orbit, target, mechanism pose, price-tag opacity, sway). `setView` blends every field over ~0.9 s; with reduced motion it snaps.
- The "Your logo here" view frames the sponsor panel using the model's `panel` anchor when there is one. The panel is drawn by `assets/js/sponsor-panel.js` on a 1024×1366 canvas: every supporter at one size (long names wrap onto two lines rather than shrink), and a typed name in a highlighted slot on top.
- Price tags are placed beside their part and pushed apart when they would overlap each other or another part's dot, and kept 44 px from the top and bottom (phones: 40 px and 48 px) so they clear the corner labels. A leader runs from the part's dot to the tag, wherever the tag was pushed. Each hotspot's `ref` is its balloon: an item number from `ITEM` in `main.js` (derived from the parts list order) or a sheet number. `onHotspot(h, { keyboard })` tells the page whether a tag was activated from the keyboard, so focus follows to the parts list only then.
- `onStage(id)` reports each finished build stage — `'frame'`, `'drive'`, `'electronics'`, `'elevator'`, `'effector'` (the labelled pauses of `buildRobotAsync`), then `'intake'`, `'shading'` (after the shader warm-up) and `'done'` (a custom model reports `'model'`, then `'done'`) — which drives the "Regenerating · n/7" status line. `onCamera(az, el)` reports the camera's azimuth and elevation in whole degrees, only when one of them changes.
- The robot assembles as it scrolls into view (once; a deep link that lands on it plays the short timed version): while the viewport rises into the screen, the parts converge and the camera swings about 20° (`ENTER_SWING`) into its datum view with the scroll (quart-out of how far it has entered, from geometry cached on resize, never read per frame); once the whole viewport is on screen it stays assembled whatever the scroll does. If the view is already in place on its first frame, the parts converge over 1.1 s instead. A tab picked during the scrub tweens on from there and ends it for good. Not with reduced motion (assembled at the datum view), and not if another tab was picked first.
- Start-up is spread over idle time so scrolling stays smooth: the engine starts once the main thread is quiet after `#robot` comes within a viewport, the robot is built in chunks (`buildRobotAsync` in `robot-model.js`), and the shaders are compiled before the first visible frame (in parallel where the GPU supports it, otherwise a few at a time).
- The canvas is sized by a `ResizeObserver`, the pixel ratio is capped (1.5 on phones, 2 otherwise), phones get a lighter model (`detail: 'low'`), and the render loop only runs while `#robot` is on screen and the tab is visible.
- No WebGL: a photo is shown instead, the price tab lists the price tags as buttons and the logo tab draws the sponsor panel in 2D, so the name preview still works. No JavaScript: the photo and all three tab texts are shown.

## Performance & accessibility notes

- Three.js is vendored (`assets/vendor/three`, r169, MIT) and only requested when you scroll near the robot section, so the hero doesn't pay for 3D (even the WebGL support check waits until then). There is one WebGL canvas on the page.
- Web fonts never block the first paint: Geist and Geist Mono load in the background in one request (`media="print"` swap, `display=swap`), and Noto Sans TC is only requested, the same way, when the page is shown in Chinese.
- Photos are WebP (~1.6 MB total) and all lazy-loaded; the hero shows only the studio render (one WebP, 150 KB, 70 KB on phones, `fetchpriority="high"`) and the crest mark (16 KB, also the nav logo). They're small originals, so no photo is shown wider than about 1.3× its native width.
- Motion never holds content back: every hidden starting state and every scroll timeline hangs off `html.mo`, which the head script only sets without reduced motion, and a 3 s backstop removes it if `motion.js` hasn't started (so a failed script can't leave blocks invisible). Without JavaScript, in print and with reduced motion the final page paints first. The scroll-driven parts (`html.sda`, set where `animation-timeline: view()` is supported) are linework only, never a fact; text is hidden at most briefly by its one-shot entrance (a record cell 750 ms, a headline line about 1.2 s). In-page jumps (`html.is-jumping`), deep links and Back / Forward land on a finished page: scroll-linked linework is mapped to the position, and records, headlines and chapter labels reached on the way are simply there.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` turns off camera sway, spinning parts, view transitions, the robot's entry scrub and assembly intro (it is assembled at its datum view), the hero's settle and callout draw, every scroll-linked drawing (the BOM and budget drawn), the headline rises, odometers and row-by-row records, the reveal fades, the pledge flight, quantity roll, arming and note, the marquee (a manual scroller, no Pause button), the map's arc flight and halo pulses, the season clock's blink, smooth scrolling (native, instant anchors), and the slide of the tab selection. What stays, as instant state changes: the record row pick, the card border, the budget's lit dimension, the tab selection's position, the view label, the nav's current link and the cross-probed cart balloon. Switching it on while the page is open stops the motion (the 3D robot included), removes any rolling wheels or flying balloon, and stops the smooth scrolling; switching it off starts the photo strip.
- Nothing moves on its own for more than 5 s without a way to stop it (WCAG 2.2.2). The scroll-linked linework (the BOM, the budget, the robot's entry scrub) is driven by your scroll and stops the instant scrolling stops; the one-shot entrances (the hero's settle and callouts, headline rises, odometers, records) finish within about 2.5 s of the page opening or 1.2 s of arriving and never repeat; the card border, the row pick and the cross-probe exist only while hovered or focused; the tab selection, the budget's re-measure and the pledge chain finish within about 1 s of the click. The photo strip, the only endless motion, has a Pause / Play button and pauses while hovered and off screen; the map halos pulse twice and the season clock blinks three times once it is on screen; the 3D robot's idle sway, elevator cycle and spinning rollers ease to rest about 5 s after the last drag, tab change or arrival in view.
- Numbers already on screen when the page opens (the hero scoreboard) never roll; only numbers scrolled into view do, and their real value stays in the page (the odometer is an `aria-hidden` overlay). Keyboard focus that reaches a block (or a season's record) before its reveal shows it at once. Every new motion node (the nav's reading line, wheels, the flying balloon, the tab selection) is `aria-hidden` and never focusable.

### Smooth scrolling

On desktop (a fine pointer that can hover, no reduced motion, no data-saver) `motion.js` loads [Lenis](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis) 1.3.26 (vendored in `assets/vendor/lenis`, imported through the import map) as soon as the page is interactive and the browser has a quiet moment (it is `modulepreload`ed on desktops only), and the mouse wheel glides (`lerp` 0.1). Wheel targets are kept on whole pixels: Lenis 1.3.26 never finishes a glide that ends on exactly half a pixel. It drives the real window scroll, so sticky elements, every observer, find-in-page, back / forward and deep links like `?lang=zh#budget` behave as usual. Touch screens never load it.

- Every in-page link goes through `goTo()` (`motion.js`): a short eased glide (0.6–1.2 s, by distance) that lands the section under the nav, updates the URL hash (back returns to where you were) and moves keyboard focus to it. The price tags use it too (the BOM row is centred) and the camera tag jumps to the budget. Without Lenis (phones, reduced motion) the browser scrolls (smooth, or instant with reduced motion), and the hash and focus still follow. If something above the target changes size on the way (an image, the robot's no-WebGL stand-in), the landing is corrected at the end.
- The keyboard and focus win: Page Up / Down, Home, End, Space and the arrow keys, and focus moving by Tab, stop a glide and scroll natively. The phone menu keeps its own scrolling (`data-lenis-prevent`).
